## Addressing Relevant Implications

### Accessibility

Throughout the process of making the web app, I made sure to adress the Accessaibility Implication in multiple aspects, such as design features, layot considerations, and HTML structuring. One design features that I made to adress the implication of accessability was to make my web app follow a logical and concise structure. This is evident in the two hemishperes of my web app, the regular user side, and the admin side. In the regular user aspect of the site, you can only go 1 page deep past the homepage, and can always clearly have a way back to the home page. This means people won't be confused by my site, and won't go so far deep that when they need to get back to a specific page, it is always at most 2 clicks away, and very easy to remember. This makes my site accessable, as it means that all types of people with different tech abilities can confidently use my site, and wont be confused by millions of pages. When I was considering the layout of the web app, I made sure to make my page layouts as clear and obvious to the users what everything is, while still maintaning a professional look for the app. This is evident in multiple aspects of my site, such as the admin home page, which shows clearly which buttons do what, while still looking as professional as possible, and not making my site be oversimplified and cluttered. This helps my site be accessable, as it means that users can be confident about what they are doing, even with little to no explaination on how to use the app. When I was coding the webpages, I made sure to adhere to HTML accessability standards, by using semantic tags and classes/ids, so people who use a screen reader don't constantly hear "div, div, div, div", but can tell what kind of section the page is. This helps add to the accessability of the site, as it means that people who use a screen reader can interact more confidently with my app, making it accessable to them. Overall, I considered different groups of people, and their different skills and weaknesses when navigating online web services, and did my best to account and accomodate for these, so my site can be used by all, regardless of any online disabilities they might have.
